# Exercise 3: Seneca Academic Integrity Policy

## Purpose

To maintain a high standard of academic integrity across the Seneca community and provide the foundation for research, teaching, learning and working practices.

## Scope

This policy applies to current students and employees.

## Key Definitions

### Academic Integrity

> Academic integrity is a commitment, even in the face of adversity, to six fundamental values: **honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility and courage**.

These values help ensure that academic credentials accurately reflect students' demonstrated knowledge and abilities.

### Cheating

**Cheating** means obtaining or attempting to obtain credit for work or improvement in performance evaluation through dishonest or deceptive means.

Examples include:

* Self-plagiarism
* Impersonation
* Falsification
* Taking individual credit for group work
* Unauthorized collaborations or aids
* Undeclared AI use

### Contract Cheating

**Contract cheating** occurs when a student submits academic work created by a third party as if it were their own for academic credit.

### Falsification/Fabrication

**Falsification or fabrication** involves misrepresenting academic work, withholding or forging documentation, or creating, altering, or reporting false or inaccurate information to gain an academic advantage.

### Impersonation

**Impersonation** means taking a test, examination, or other assessment on behalf of another individual.

### Plagiarism

**Plagiarism** is using another individual, group, or organization's work, including words, images, ideas, logic, phrases, signatures, or computations, and presenting it as one's own without properly citing the source.

### Self-Plagiarism

**Self-plagiarism** is the unauthorized reuse of one's own work from previous assignments.

### Unauthorized Aid

**Unauthorized aid** means using non-approved supports to complete coursework or assessments, including peer collaboration, artificial intelligence technology, the internet, or smartphones.

### Undeclared AI Use

**Undeclared AI use** means using artificial intelligence to generate part or all of an assessment without a disclosure statement explaining how and to what extent the technology was used.

### Academic Integrity Violation

An **academic integrity violation** is an attempt to gain an unfair advantage in a formal assessment, such as coursework or exams.

# Policy

## 1. General

1. Seneca upholds a learning community that values **academic integrity, honesty, fairness, trust, respect, responsibility and courage**.
2. Seneca is committed to delivering high-quality education and teaching excellence while supporting a positive, equitable and inclusive learning environment.

## 2. Academic Integrity Program

1. Academic integrity is fundamental to ensuring that academic credentials granted by Seneca accurately demonstrate the knowledge and learning of students.
2. The program requires policies, procedures, faculty development and training, student training sessions, and student life programming to be aligned with Seneca's approaches to academic integrity.

## 3. Responsibilities

1. **Seneca** is responsible for providing information and resources to students about academic integrity, its importance, how to avoid violations, and how to receive support if a violation occurs.
2. **Students** are responsible for informing faculty of circumstances affecting their academic performance as soon as they are known so that alternate arrangements can be considered.
3. Students who witness or suspect an academic integrity violation can report it according to the procedures in Appendix A.

## 4. Sanctions

1. Academic integrity sanctions are applied according to the **severity of the violation**.
2. Before final decisions are made or sanctions are applied, the student's record should be consulted to determine whether there are aggravating or mitigating factors.
3. If a suspected academic integrity violation is also connected to a violation of the Student Code of Conduct or another Seneca policy, the matter may be investigated through the applicable procedures.

# Appendix A: Academic Integrity Procedure

## Information Gathering and Assessment

| Action                                                                                                           | Duration                                            | Responsibility                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Report the witnessing or suspicion of an academic integrity violation to the designate in the school/program. | As soon as possible, within a reasonable timeframe. | Faculty, invigilator, student, support staff |
| 2. Inform the student of the suspected academic integrity offence and discuss the situation informally.          | As soon as possible, within a reasonable timeframe. | Faculty, invigilator, support staff          |
| 3. Collect and retain the assessment and related evidence.                                                       | Within five days of the reported violation.         | Faculty, invigilator, support staff          |
| 3a. If no offence occurred, inform the student and take no further action.                                       | Within two Seneca business days.                    | Faculty                                      |
| 3b. If an offence occurred, inform the student and begin the formal process.                                     | Within five Seneca business days.                   | Faculty                                      |

## Formal Reporting of Academic Integrity Violation

1. Complete an **Academic Integrity Report** within five Seneca business days of the alleged violation.
2. Provide the report and a blank **Student Response to the Academic Integrity Report** to the student and specified school liaison.
3. The student may submit a completed response to the chair or specified school liaison.
4. An **Academic Integrity Committee** meeting may be convened.
5. The committee provides its decision in writing.
6. If no violation occurred, the report is destroyed.
7. If a violation occurred, sanctions are imposed according to the applicable sanctions guidelines.
8. The student may appeal the decision according to Seneca's **Academic Appeals Policy**.

## Sanctions

Seneca's Academic Integrity Program establishes sanctions according to the nature and extent of an academic integrity offence.

### Types of Sanctions

Sanctions may include:

* A verbal or written warning
* Completion of an academic integrity assignment or course
* A make-up assignment or rewriting of an assignment or examination
* A lower grade on an assessment
* A lower grade in the course
* Failure in the course
* Suspension from Seneca
* Permanent expulsion in specified circumstances
* Other appropriate penalties

### Factors for Consideration

The Academic Integrity Committee may consider:

* The extent of the violation
* The student's level of academic experience
* The value of the assessment
* Extenuating circumstances
* Whether the student accepts responsibility
* Prior incidents

### Recording of Sanctions

Academic sanctions may be recorded on the student's account or record.

Possible indicators include:

* **Academic Integrity Warning (AIW)**
* **Academic Integrity Offence (AIO)**
* **Academic Integrity Suspension (AIS)**
